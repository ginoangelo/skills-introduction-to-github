# Aimed AI video prompts

Prompts for Google AI Studio (Veo for video, the image model for stills). Each one makes a short, realistic clip with emotion only. The Aimed videos in [`video/`](video/) show the real app, so the two are joined in CapCut.

## Setup

1. Open [Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com/) and choose Veo for video, or the image model for stills.
2. Set the aspect ratio to **9:16 (vertical)** if it's offered.
3. Paste a prompt, generate 2 to 4 versions, and download the best one.

## Rules for every clip

- **Never show the phone screen.** AI video garbles text on screens. The Aimed videos show the real app.
- **No text, no logos, no brand names** in the AI clip.
- **Made-up people only.** Never prompt for a real person, celebrity, or someone you know.
- **Label it.** Turn on TikTok's "AI-generated content" label, or Instagram's "AI info" label, when a post includes an AI clip.

## Get better results

1. **Use a recurring character.** Paste the same description into every prompt so the same "person" appears across videos. That turns one-off clips into a series people follow.

    ```
    CHARACTER: Maya, a woman in her early twenties with shoulder-length curly dark hair,
    small gold hoop earrings, wearing an oversized lavender hoodie. Expressive face,
    quick to overthink, funny.
    ```

2. **Make a still first, then animate it.** Create the image of Maya in her bedroom with the image model, then use it as the starting frame in Veo. Faces and rooms stay much more consistent.
3. **Add camera language.** Phrases like "35mm lens, f/1.8, eye level" or "handheld, slight sway" make clips look filmed rather than generated.
4. **Generate several versions.** Hands and faces vary a lot between takes. Keep the best one.
5. **Match the brand colors.** End every clip in violet and peach light, so the cut into a Aimed video feels like one piece.
6. **Fix one thing at a time.** If a result looks off, add one detail ("fairy lights on the wall," "static shot") and regenerate, so you know what changed it.

## The first four prompts

### 1. The overthinking clip

Use it to open the 3 a.m. overthinking video.

```
Vertical 9:16 video, 8 seconds, cinematic and realistic.
A young woman in her early twenties lies in bed in a dark bedroom at 3 a.m.
Her face is lit only by the glow of her phone, a soft violet and warm peach light.
She scrolls, stops, rereads something, and frowns slightly, then sighs.
Slow push-in from a medium shot to a close-up on her face.
Shallow depth of field, gentle film grain, quiet and moody.
Audio: soft room tone, a faint phone tap, one quiet sigh. No music.
Do not show the phone screen. No text, no logos, no captions.
```

### 2. The group chat reaction

Use it before the group chat video.

```
Vertical 9:16 video, 8 seconds, realistic, natural light.
Three friends in their twenties sit close together on a couch in a cozy apartment at night,
huddled around one phone. One gasps and points at the screen, one covers her mouth,
one shakes her head and laughs. Warm lamp light with soft violet accents.
Handheld camera, slight movement, like a friend filming.
Audio: muffled excited chatter and laughter, no clear words.
Do not show the phone screen. No text, no logos, no brand names.
```

### 3. The relief moment

Use it at the end of any video.

```
Vertical 9:16 video, 6 seconds, realistic.
Close-up of a young man sitting on his bed at night, holding his phone.
He reads something on the screen, his shoulders relax, and he lets out a small relieved smile.
Soft violet and peach light from the phone, dark background.
Slow, steady camera. Calm mood.
Audio: quiet room tone, a soft exhale.
Do not show the phone screen. No text, no logos.
```

### 4. Story background still

Use the image model. These make backgrounds for made-up story posts.

```
Vertical 9:16 photo, dreamy sunset sky over a city rooftop, warm peach fading into violet,
soft clouds, no people, no text, no logos. Calm and slightly melancholy mood,
like a background someone would post on a story with a cryptic caption.
```

## Five new scenes

### 5. Typing, then deleting

Shows the problem before Aimed solves it.

```
Vertical 9:16, 8 seconds, realistic close-up of a young woman's thumbs over a phone
in bed at night. She types quickly, pauses, deletes everything, types again, stops,
and drops the phone on the blanket. Violet and peach phone glow on her hands.
Static shot, shallow depth of field. Audio: soft keyboard taps, a frustrated exhale.
Do not show the screen. No text, no logos.
```

### 6. The double take on the street

Daytime, to break up the night look.

```
Vertical 9:16, 8 seconds, realistic. A young man walks down a sunny city sidewalk
looking at his phone, stops mid-step, scrolls back up, and stares, eyebrows raised.
People pass blurred in the background. Handheld tracking shot from the front.
Audio: street ambience, his footsteps stop. Do not show the screen. No text, no logos.
```

### 7. The overreaction versus the real read

A comedy split. Make both clips, then put them side by side in CapCut with the labels on screen.

```
Clip A, labeled "What I think it means": Vertical 9:16, 6 seconds. A young woman sits
by a rainy window at night, dramatic slow motion, single tear, moody blue light,
like a movie breakup scene. Audio: heavy rain. No text, no logos.

Clip B, labeled "What it actually means": Vertical 9:16, 6 seconds. The same woman,
same room, lights on, eating cereal and laughing at her phone, totally relaxed.
Audio: crunching, a small laugh. No text, no logos.
```

### 8. The coworker read

### 9. First-person POV in bed

POV is one of TikTok's most native formats.

```
Vertical 9:16, 8 seconds, first-person point of view lying in bed at night, holding a
phone above the face with both hands. The phone glows violet and peach. The hands
tilt the phone closer, then lower it slowly onto the chest. Ceiling with fairy lights
softly out of focus. Audio: room tone, a long sigh. Do not show the screen. No text.
```

## One clip for each Instagram screenshot

Each prompt below matches one image in [`social/`](social/). Maya's description is built into every prompt, so each block can be pasted on its own and she stays the same person.

**Put each one together in the Instagram Reels editor or CapCut:**

1. Generate the clip (8 seconds) and download the best version.
2. Add the matching screenshot right after it for 3 seconds. For Reels, use the 9:16 version from [`social/tiktok/`](social/tiktok/), so it fills the screen.
3. Add a sound, paste the caption, and turn on the AI label.

## Other formats to try

- **Decode of the day.** A daily 10-second series: one made-up post, one read. Repetition builds a habit.
- **Stitch and duet bait.** Post a made-up cryptic post with "Stitch this with your guess." People make content for you.
- **App Store preview video.** A 15 to 30 second video for your store page. Apple requires real app footage only, so use the Aimed videos, not AI clips.
- **Creator kits.** Send partner creators a made-up post, its Aimed read, and three opening lines. They film their reaction.
