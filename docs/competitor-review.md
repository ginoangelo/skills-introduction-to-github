# Subtext competitor review

How the "Subtext" and text-decoder apps on the App Store compare to yours, what their reviewers say, and how to stand apart. Checked 2026-11-21 from the US App Store listings and web search. Ratings and prices change, so recheck before launch.

## The headline: the name is taken, four times over

At least four App Store apps already use "Subtext" for nearly the same idea, plus several websites:

Websites using the name too: [subtext.it](https://subtext.it/), [subtext.ing](https://subtext.ing/), [subtext.chat](https://subtext.chat/).

**Why this matters.** Someone who hears about you on TikTok and searches "Subtext" finds four other apps first. Their reviews, good or bad, get mixed up with yours. A trademark dispute after launch would force a rename when you have users. Rename before launch, and keep "Read between the lines" as the tagline.

**Name ideas to check.** Each one still needs a search of the App Store, Google Play, domains, and the USPTO trademark database:

- **Clocked** ("I got it clocked")
- **Undertone**
- **Aimed** ("Was that aimed at me?")
- **Readout**
- **Overthought**
- **Vague** (owning the "vague post" moment)

## What their reviewers say

There are few reviews in this category so far, so these are signals, not proof.

**What people like**

- "Stop overthinking" and "no more screenshot group chats at 2 a.m." land well. ([SubText AI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/subtext-ai/id6747407704))
- Feeling human, not robotic. One reviewer said ChatGPT felt "robotic and soulless" and this didn't. ([Anton Licht's Subtext](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ai-text-message-subtext/id6736559861))
- Neurodivergent users find tone help valuable. A reviewer with autism called it "genius" and "an untapped market." (same listing)
- Calibrated language. Keller Tech's listing leads with "a reading, not a verdict" and "it can be wrong."

**What people complain about**

- **Paying before trying.** "There should be at least one free trial example. I bought this out of desperation." ([Anton Licht's Subtext](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ai-text-message-subtext/id6736559861))
- **Forced subscriptions.** A Google Play reviewer of Subtext (subtext.it) said you can't do anything without subscribing, according to search results.
- **Privacy surprise.** The same reviewer pointed out that the consent text says screenshots go to the app's AI provider, Anthropic.
- **Weekly billing, charges after cancelling, hidden limits.** These are the most common complaints across AI texting apps, such as [TextAI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/textai-ai-texting-assistant/id1659814788) and [TextGuard](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/textguard-ai/id6737261756?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone).

## How yours compares

| | Your app | The others |
| --- | --- | --- |

| **The question it answers** | "Is this post about me?" | "What did they mean in this text?" |

## Where you're different, and how to say it

**Your lane: posts, not texts.** Every competitor reads private conversations. None of them talk about stories, song captions, close friends, vague posts, or subliminals. That's yours.

> They decode your DMs. We decode the posts that might be about you.

Lean into it everywhere:

- **App Store subtitle:** "Is this post about you?"
- **Search keywords:** story meaning, vague post, subliminal, caption meaning, is this about me, song caption. Skip "text decoder." It's crowded.
- **Screenshots and ads:** stories, captions, and song stickers, not chat bubbles. That already matches your ads and videos.

**Your second edge: honesty as a feature.**

> We'll tell you when it's not about you.

The "Not enough to tell" and "Unlikely" answers, the evidence, and "other readings" set you apart from apps that promise to detect manipulation. Say it in the store listing and the first screen.

## Ways to improve, in order

1. **Rename before launch.** This is the highest-impact item.
2. **Show a free read before any signup or payment.** Run the example decode automatically on first launch, so nobody pays out of desperation. You already have the example built.
3. **Make billing a trust signal.** No weekly plans, which you already avoid. Remind people the day before the yearly trial ends. Put "Cancel any time in Settings" on the paywall, which you already have. Competitors' worst reviews come from billing surprises.
4. **Be upfront about the AI provider.** Your consent screen already names Claude, by Anthropic. Keep that wording plain, so nobody finds out later and feels misled.
5. **Add more ways to bring in a post.** Competitors accept long scrolling screenshots and screen recordings. Supporting long screenshots helps with comment sections, which also fits Pro's whole-thread feature.
6. **Decide on reply help.** Every texting competitor suggests replies. Your current rule is no advice. Options:
    - Keep it out and own "we read, you decide."
    - Add one optional, calm next step, like Keller Tech's single prompt.
7. **Ask for reviews at the right moment.** After someone rates a read as accurate, use Apple's built-in review prompt. Reply to every review, as Anton Licht does.
8. **Set the age rating on purpose.** AuraBionics rates its app 18+. Yours reads social posts, which is where teen drama lives. A 17+ rating lowers bullying risk and review trouble, but costs some young users. Decide before submitting.
9. **Consider accessibility as a second audience.** Reviewers with autism found tone reading genuinely helpful. A short section in the store listing and on your site could reach people who need it most.
